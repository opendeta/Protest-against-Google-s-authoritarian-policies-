**Our Devices Are NOT Google Private Prisons!**

We all chose Android for one simple reason: **Freedom.** We loved the fact that we didn't need anyone's permission to build an app or install it. But today, Google is silently pulling the plug on that freedom. Under the fake banner of "security," Google is systematically engineering Android to kill third-party app installations and side-loading once and for all.

If you are an independent developer or a power user, you can already feel Google's trap tightening around your neck.

Every new Android update makes it harder for users to install apps from independent stores or direct links. Google is transforming their "Play Protect" into an aggressive gatekeeper that treats every single APK from outside their monopoly as a toxic threat. They flash terrifying, non-skippable warning screens to ordinary users, practically forcing them to believe that if a developer didn't pay Google's corporate toll, their software cannot be trusted. They want to turn Android into Apple’s iOS—a closed, locked-down cage where Google decides what runs on your phone.

Think about the sheer hypocrisy. Google wants us to spend our nights writing code, testing APIs, and solving problems, only to hand over total control to them. Through silent background updates via Google Play Services, they can modify how your device handles external packages overnight. They are actively trying to build a world where downloading an app from a browser or an open-source store becomes completely impossible. They want to strip away the absolute core of Android—the right to own your hardware.

We refuse to live in Google's digital dictatorship. This repository stands firmly against this corporate lockdown. We host our creations in our own independent stores because we believe that when a user buys a phone, they own it completely—from the screen to the kernel. Google has no right to block our code just because it doesn't feed their advertising machine.

To every developer who is watching Google slowly murder the open-source spirit of Android: if we don't speak up now, there won't be an open platform left to build on tomorrow.
